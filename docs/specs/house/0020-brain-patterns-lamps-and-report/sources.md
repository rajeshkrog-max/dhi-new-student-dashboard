# Sources for the Brain's patterns · spec 0020 · found 2026-10-22

_The scope says a pattern without a checked source does not ship. These were found by a web search and read as abstracts and summaries only. The DHI team has not read them. `PATTERN_SOURCES_CONFIRMED` stays false, and every hit carries a draft note, until the team confirms or removes each one. The links were returned by the search and have not been opened by the team._

**What the sources can and cannot support.** Every pattern is an observation about one student's own days, worded "in your own days, these tend to come together". None of the sources tests the exact rule, and none of them licenses the words "causes", "because" or advice. The honest summary is that the evidence for the direction of each pattern is indirect, and the evidence for the meals chain is the weakest.

| Pattern | What the research says (indirect) | Source found | Limits the team should weigh |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mood_habits_quiet`: heavy days, habits not ticked, a quieter next day | Low mood in young people goes with lower school involvement and lower grades, and activity based treatment (behavioral activation) rests on the idea that withdrawing from routines and activity keeps low mood going | A study of 155 pairs of adolescent friends, where more depressive symptoms went with lower grades through lower school involvement (primary study; the search did not tie it to one link, so the team should locate it); Martin and Oliver, *Behavioral activation for children and adolescents: a systematic review of progress and promise* (listed as 2018, [Bath](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/behavioral-activation-for-children-and-adolescents-a-systematic-r/)); a 2017 systematic review and meta-analysis of behavioral activation for depression in young people in *Psychology and Psychotherapy* ([PubMed 28299896](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28299896/); the authors and title are for the team to confirm) | Mostly cross sectional, so direction is not shown. The behavioral activation reviews are about treatment, mostly small trials, and say the evidence is promising but not conclusive. None tests a day to day chain from mood to next day activity |
| `steady_rhythm`: a steady rhythm and papers sat (supporting) | Study habits, skills and attitudes predict academic performance, and missing a single day does not stop a habit forming | Credé and Kuncel (2008), *Study Habits, Skills, and Attitudes: The Third Pillar Supporting Collegiate Academic Performance*, Perspectives on Psychological Science 3, 425 to 453 (DOI 10.1111/j.1745-6924.2008.00089.x); Lally et al. (2010), *How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world*, European Journal of Social Psychology 40, 998 to 1009 (DOI 10.1002/ejsp.674) | Credé and Kuncel studied college students, and a later review reports the study time and achievement link as small to moderate. Lally et al. had 96 adults and a self reported habit measure. Neither tests the study hour or paper completion. The "one missed day does not matter" finding is why the wording never punishes a miss |
| `packaged_quiet`: packaged or fried days, a quieter next day (**off**) | Higher intake of ultra processed food goes with higher odds of depressive and anxiety symptoms in observational studies | Ultra-Processed Food Consumption and Mental Health: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Observational Studies ([PMC9268228](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9268228/), 17 studies, mostly adults); a review of 20 studies in children and adolescents (reported on the same search, not opened) | Observational and mostly adult. "Packaged or fried" is a proxy for "ultra processed". **Nothing found links it to study time or to missing a paper**, and this is for students under 18. This is why the chain is off |
| expression note | Writing about feelings has a small positive effect on emotional distress, problem behavior, social adjustment and school participation in adolescents, and no clear effect on grades | Travagin, Margola and Revenson (2015), *How effective are expressive writing interventions for adolescents? A meta-analytic review*, Clinical Psychology Review (21 studies, ages 10 to 18, pooled g = 0.127; [summary](https://www.ojp.gov/library/publications/how-effective-are-expressive-writing-interventions-adolescents-meta-analytic)) | A small effect, and an intervention (guided writing sessions), not a student's free posts. The note therefore claims only that "some people find that putting a feeling into words helps" and asks for nothing |
| support offer | Not a research claim. It names a service | Tele-MANAS, the government of India's free tele mental health helpline: 14416 (also 1-800-891-4416), all day and night, in many languages ([Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/mental-health/tele-manas), government statements reported by several news sources) | Confirm the number on the official Tele-MANAS page before real students see it. The offer's thresholds are a judgement of the builder, not a validated screen: it is a prompt to talk to someone, never a measure of anyone's wellbeing |

**Not used, though searched:** breakfast and academic performance (Adolphus and colleagues' reviews in Leeds: a positive link in children, little research on adolescents), sleep and mood in adolescents (Short and colleagues, 2020, 73 studies, a link between less sleep and mood problems), and sleep regularity and depressive symptoms (a 6 month study of 46 adolescents). They would support a sleep or breakfast pattern, which this pass does not build.

**Decision needed from the DHI team, pattern by pattern:** read the source, then keep, reword or remove it. If a source is rejected, the pattern id is removed from `PATTERN_IDS` in `brainCodes.ts` and its words from `brain-words.ts`; the copy test and the rules test then fail until both are updated, which is the point.
